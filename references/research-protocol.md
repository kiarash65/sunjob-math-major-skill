# Research Protocol

Use current research when facts can change.

## Source hierarchy
1. سازمان سنجش / official admission documents and announcements
2. Ministry of Science and official university sources
3. Statistics Center of Iran and official government datasets
4. official professional organizations
5. reputable labor-market reports
6. strong secondary analysis

## Current-cycle admission research
For questions such as "what can I get with rank X?", do not start with a generic cutoff search. First identify the minimum variables that materially change feasibility: current rank type, quota/region, exam group, target major/family, target universities, geography constraints, and relevant course/period constraints.

The current result used for selection may contain multiple rank/score fields. Do not silently substitute national rank for rank in quota/region or vice versa. Use the exact field shown on the student's current result.

Search current official documents first. Secondary sites can be used for discovery and historical context, but they cannot override a current official rule.

## Historical cutoff discipline
Historical cutoff figures are evidence about past admission behavior only. Label the year and context. Never say "with this rank you will definitely get X" based on historical cutoffs. Prefer calibrated language such as "historically competitive", "reach", "plausible", or "low-confidence estimate" when appropriate.

## When evidence is incomplete
If current primary data cannot be retrieved, say so. Do not fill the gap with invented numbers. Continue with the parts that can be answered reliably: fit, decision structure, required variables, and what source/data is needed next.

## Career and test research
Use authoritative occupational/career frameworks where useful. O*NET, for example, separates interests, abilities, skills, work styles, work values, education/experience, work activities, work context and labor-market information; this supports a multi-factor interpretation rather than mapping one test result directly to one major.
