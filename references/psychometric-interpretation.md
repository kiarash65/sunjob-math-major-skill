# Psychometric Interpretation Discipline

Treat every assessment as evidence about a construct, not a verdict about identity or destiny.

## Core principle
A test result can increase or decrease confidence in a hypothesis; it cannot manufacture certainty where conversation and behavior provide no support.

## By test

### Holland / RIASEC
- Use interest patterns to generate and examine occupational hypotheses.
- Do not equate a high code with one major.
- A high I does not mean "must do computer science" — it means analytical/investigative work environments are more likely to fit.
- Low scores are not deficits; they indicate lower priority, not inability.

### Work Values
- Use values to evaluate work environments and trade-offs.
- Do not assume they predict one specific occupation.
- High security + high autonomy = tension, not impossibility. Investigate which environments satisfy both partially.

### Blind Spots
- The score is a tendency indicator, not a diagnosis.
- High rank-effect means the student's preferences may shift when the rank is revealed — this is a bias to watch, not a personality trait.
- Translate the result into concrete decision consequences, not labels.

### Career Self-Efficacy
- Interpret as confidence in performing/managing career tasks, not raw ability, intelligence, or future success.
- Low confidence ≠ low potential.
- High confidence in the wrong direction is as risky as low confidence.

### MBTI
- Use cautiously as descriptive context, never as deterministic career assignment.
- An INTJ tag does not prescribe engineering; it describes a cognitive preference pattern.

### Big Five
- Use as a supplementary personality lens.
- Do not make deterministic career claims from trait levels.
- Focus on how traits interact with work environments, not on "which job fits this personality."

## When tests conflict
- Prefer an explanation that preserves both signals.
- Investigate the real-world behavior/environment that could reconcile them.
- Example: Holland I-high + Work Values team-first ≠ contradiction. Analytical work can happen in collaborative settings (data science teams, research groups, agile development).

## Common misuses to avoid
- Mapping a single test score directly to a single major.
- Saying "your test says you should study X."
- Using test results to override what the student says about their own interests.
- Treating psychometric instruments as more valid than direct conversation evidence.
